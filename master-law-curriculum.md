# The "Top 5" Self-Taught JD-Equivalent Curriculum
## 34 Courses · Built from Harvard, Yale, Stanford, Columbia & Chicago curricula

**How to use:** One class-week = read the rules/text first → brief 2–3 cases → do practice drills.
All cases are free on courtlistener.com. UCC, MPC, FRE, FRCP, and statutes are free online.
End each course with a timed 3-hour practice exam graded against a model answer.

# APPENDIX A — Self-Grading Protocol

1. Find an old exam with a model answer: search `"course name" exam "model answer" site:.edu`
2. Write it under timed 3-hour conditions, closed book unless the course permitted open rules
3. Grade against the model, deducting for missed issue-spotting (the biggest self-taught failure mode), not just wrong analysis
4. Common errors to check for: missed 403 balancing (Evidence), missed Erie issues (Civ Pro), Crawford errors (Evidence), RAP mistakes (Property), proximate-cause shortcuts (Torts)

# APPENDIX B — Licensure Reminder

This curriculum is education, not licensure. To become a lawyer without a JD, you must be in a "reading the law" state (California, Virginia, Vermont, Washington, and a few others), register with that state's bar, and complete a multi-year apprenticeship under a lawyer or judge. Plan electives around that state's bar subjects if licensure is your goal.
